About

I've spent the better part of a decade asking what AI can actually do when you point it at genuinely hard problems. Then I go find out.

That curiosity has dragged me across a lot of territory: modelling curiosity itself in reinforcement learning agents (yes, that's a real thing and yes, it's as meta as it sounds), clustering spike trains from olfactory neurons, building knowledge graphs for cyber-physical systems and now designing production-grade multi-agent orchestration pipelines that need to actually hold together under pressure. The domains keep changing. The obsession with systems that think doesn't.

My background is genuinely weird in a good way. Aerospace engineering taught me how physical systems fail. A master's in mathematical modelling gave me the language to describe why. Seven-plus years at Thoughtworks gave me a place to do real research and ship real things at the same time. That's meant working on RL agents learning to navigate and collaborate, robots finding their way through environments using SLAM and sensor fusion in ROS and Gazebo, graph neural networks making sense of relational structure between proteins and drugs and agentic architectures that need to plan, execute and recover gracefully. The through-line across all of it has been the same question: how do you build something that reasons?

I also teach, because explaining something badly is the fastest way to discover you didn't understand it. I've run sessions on ML mathematics and stochastic optimisation at Savitribai Phule Pune University (SPPU). There's something genuinely satisfying about watching a hard idea finally click for someone.

Outside work, things get interesting. I write, not just technical articles but poetry too, Hindi and Urdu, because some things don't translate cleanly into equations. I make music and spend real time on it, composition, production, the whole process. I swim regularly because forty minutes in a pool is the only reliable way I've found to shut the brain up long enough to actually think. I read a lot of technical stuff happily. Non-technical books I also genuinely want to read, I just happen to be on page 47 of the same one for the past three months. Documentaries and docuseries pick up the slack there, along with a probably unhealthy amount of YouTube rabbit holes on topics like ML theory, philosophy, history or whatever pulls. And I play PS4 with the kind of commitment I refuse to be embarrassed about.

The curiosity really doesn't have an off switch. Fortunately, neither does the fun.
